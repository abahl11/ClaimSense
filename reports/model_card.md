# Model card: workers' compensation claim cost model and high-cost flag

_Generated by `python -m src.pipeline --stage report`. Do not edit by hand._

## Model details

- **Cost model:** LightGBM, tuned (A, Layers 1-2: + TF-IDF/SVD); target approach *A: log cost*; 126 input columns built from structured claim fields and the free-text description. Predicts the expected (mean) ultimate cost, a typical (median-type) cost and an 80% interval (LightGBM quantile models).
- **High-cost flag:** Rank by regression model's predicted cost, calibrated with logistic (Platt) scaling; flags claims likely to reach 16,406 or more (the 90th percentile of training claims).
- **Training data:** 43,200 claims with accidents from 1988-01-01 to 2002-05-29. Code, configuration and every result are reproducible with `python -m src.pipeline --stage all` (fixed random seed 42).

## Intended use

- Decision support **at the moment a claim is reported**: a suggested initial reserve with an uncertainty range, and an early-escalation flag for claims likely to become expensive, for a claims adjuster to review.
- Portfolio-level checks for reserving (does the total of reserves track expected cost?) and driver insights for underwriting and risk & safety teams.

## Out-of-scope uses

- Automatic denial, reduction or approval of benefits, or any decision about an individual without human review.
- Pricing an individual worker or employer, or screening job applicants.
- Use on other jurisdictions, lines of business or real claims without revalidation: the model was trained on synthetic data.
- Causal conclusions: SHAP explanations describe what the model uses, not what causes costs.

## Training data

Kaggle *Actuarial Loss Estimation* `train.csv`: 54,000 synthetic workers' compensation claims, 54,000 after cleaning (0 rows dropped; invalid hours, days and wages set to missing and imputed inside the model pipeline). Known artifacts: suspected default wage values, accident times of 00:00 meaning 'unknown', and a placeholder initial-estimate value. See [data_quality_report.md](data_quality_report.md).

## Limitations

- **Synthetic data.** Relationships in this data (for example the relative costs of injury types) may not hold in real claims.
- **The largest claims are hard to foresee.** For the top 1% of test claims the model predicts 0.23 of their actual cost: most of what makes a claim huge happens after it is reported.
- **Drift.** Costs rose 3.7x over the data period; the model reflects the price level of its training data. The flag's probabilities held up on the test period (mean 31.4% vs an observed 31.0%), but calibration must be refreshed as prices move.
- **Text rules are hand-built.** The keyword taxonomy leaves 22% of descriptions without a cause label, and misspellings or new jargon will be missed.

## Ethical considerations

- Predictions affect injured workers indirectly (through reserves and claim handling). Keep a human in the loop, and never use the flag to delay or deny care.
- Protected characteristics (gender, age) are monitored group by group; gender is only used if it adds measurable accuracy, and even then not for individual decisions.
- Each prediction comes with its main drivers, so an adjuster can challenge it.

## Monitoring and maintenance

- Track predicted vs actual cost as claims close, by accident quarter, and the share of claims crossing the high-cost threshold; alert on sustained bias.
- Monitor input drift (wage and initial-estimate distributions, description vocabulary) and the taxonomy's 'other' rate.
- Retrain at least yearly (and after large price changes), refresh the probability calibration more often, and re-run the fairness checks at every retrain.
